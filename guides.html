Who can own land

Article XII, Section 7 of the 1987 Constitution is the starting line: private land may be transferred only to persons or corporations qualified to hold land of the public domain. In practice that means Filipino citizens and Philippine corporations that are at least 60% Filipino-owned.

Licensed brokers and License to Sell

RA 9646 (Real Estate Service Act) requires a PRC license to broker a sale, purchase, or lease for a fee. A salesperson may work only under a licensed broker. Ads and the office should show the broker’s license number. You can verify a PRC ID on the official PRC verification site.

PD 957 still governs subdivision lots and condominium units. The developer needs a DHSUD Certificate of Registration and a License to Sell before offering to the public. DHSUD has warned, repeatedly, not to buy from unlicensed projects. Advertisements for a licensed project should carry the project name, location, and LTS details.

Most inventory on this site is resale — a private owner, a TCT, a Deed of Absolute Sale. That is not a PD 957 public offering. When a listing is still developer inventory, we say so and we ask for the LTS before anyone pays a reservation.

Maceda Law (RA 6552) and installment buyers

If you buy a residential house or lot on installments, RA 6552 is your shield against a brutal cancellation:

  • You may pay in advance, or pay the balance in full, without extra interest on the unused term.
  • If you have paid less than two years, you generally get a 60-day grace period.
  • If you have paid at least two years, you get a 60-day grace period and, on cancellation, a cash surrender value of at least 50% of payments (plus 5% per year after five years, capped at 90%).
  • Cancellation needs a notarized notice, not a Facebook message.

PD 957 adds another layer on licensed projects: amenities that were advertised must be delivered, and buyers have refund rights when the developer fails in ways the decree covers. Do not let anyone tell you “Maceda does not apply because it is a village.” Ask counsel.

Taxes, BIR RDO 110, and the City Treasurer

General Santos titles move through the Registry of Deeds of General Santos City and taxes through BIR Revenue District Office No. 110. Zonal values for the city sit under that RDO. Always compare the deed price with the zonal value and the city assessor’s fair market value. The BIR will use the highest.

If the property is an ordinary asset (a developer, a dealer, or a seller in the real-estate business), the 6% CGT often does not apply. You may instead see creditable withholding tax and, when the VAT rules are met, 12% VAT. That changes the math. Do not use the capital-asset calculator blindly on a developer invoice.

The transfer does not finish at the notary. The BIR issues a Certificate Authorizing Registration (CAR) only after the right taxes are in. The Registry of Deeds will not transfer the TCT without it. Then you update the tax declaration at the city assessor and keep RPT current.

Agricultural land, CLOA, and “we will convert later”

A green field in Fatima or Conel is not a house-and-lot. If the TCT is agricultural, non-farm development generally needs DAR conversion and city rezoning. Conversion is a government act, not a broker promise.

CLOA and emancipation-patent lands from agrarian reform carry holding periods and transfer limits. We disclose that class of title or we do not list it as a simple residential lot.

After the 8 June 2026 earthquake

At 7:37 in the morning on 8 June 2026, a magnitude 7.8 earthquake offshore west of Maasim, Sarangani, shook General Santos at destructive intensity. PHIVOLCS tied the event to the Cotabato Trench. In this city, thousands of houses were damaged or destroyed. That is not a marketing footnote. It is why our listings say whether we walked the property afterward.

A “Inspected 2026” chip on this site means a visual walk-through by this desk. It is not a structural engineering report, not a city occupancy certificate, and not a promise the house will stand the next event. Budget for an independent civil engineer before you release a large payment.

Buyer’s checklist we actually use

Before you reserve

  • Walk the lot lines, not just the living room.
  • Ask for a certified true copy of the TCT or CCT from the Registry of Deeds.
  • Match the seller’s name and civil status to IDs and, if married, the spouse’s consent.
  • Read annotations: mortgages, lis pendens, adverse claims, Section 4 Rule 74.
  • Get latest RPT official receipts and a tax clearance path.
  • Compare asking price with RDO 110 zonal value.
  • If it is a project, demand the License to Sell.

Before you pay the balance

  • Independent engineer after June 2026, especially on older two-storeys and walk-ups.
  • Village deed of restrictions and HOA dues in writing.
  • Who pays CGT, DST, transfer tax, notarial, and broker’s commission — in the deed.
  • Pag-IBIG or bank appraisal if you are financing.
  • CAR issued, then RD transfer, then new tax declaration.
  • Keys and utility clearances only after the paper path is real.

Short answers

Is “tax declaration only” a title?

No. A tax declaration is evidence of a claim for assessment. It is not a TCT. We will list it only with that sentence in the open.

Can I buy in cash and skip the BIR?

No. An unregistered deed is a weak right against the world. The next buyer, a bank, or an heir will ask for the CAR and the new TCT.

Do I need a broker if the owner is my relative?

Not if you are not paying anyone to broker. If someone is earning a commission, RA 9646 wants that person licensed.
